It has always been customary at the
Asylum for the Insane here, as at all well.
conducted places of the sort, to have such
of the inmates as can be trusted supple.
ment the work of the paid officers. Yes.
terday afternoon a gang of men were busily
engaged assisting the gardener, when two
of them commenced what is vulgarly called
" monkeying. They kept it up for a little
while, when one of them, Joseph Burns,
struck the other, David Moore, with the
flat part of his spade on the head. Then
they stopped fooling and went to work.
Moore took no notice of the blow,
and worked for almost two hours,
talking to Burns occasionally as if
nothing had occurred. The blow left
no mark on Moore's head and nothing was
thought of the affair until the men were
taken into the institution for supper, when
Moore began to complain of his head.
Shortly afterwards he became stupid. Dr.
Reynolds examined him, but still there
were no external marks. He reported to
Dr. Russell, who also examined the man.
At that time his breathing and other symptoms-
toms indicated apoplexy. It was feared
that the blow had caused internal injuries,
and the fears proved true. In an hour the
man died, despite all that could be done for
him. Dr. Russell is of the opinion that a
blow such as was dealt Moore would never
result seriously to a man in hisright mind,
but toone whose brain was affected it would
be different. He ha ε ne doubt that death
was caused indirectly by the blow. He
notified Dr. White, coroner, of the case,
but when that gentleman heard all the par-
ticulars he decided that it was not necessary-
sary to hold an inquest.

(From our own Correspondent.),)
A. mammoth excursion, comprising the
South Grey, Centre Wellington ard West
Wellington Farmers Institutes Was made
to the Experimental Farm, Guelph, on
Friday. No less than 600 to 800 persons
were present. A couple of hours were spent
in examining the college buildings and the
outbuildings of the farm, with all of which
the visitors expressed very much satisfaction-
tion. A luncheon was then, partaken of
provided by the college.
President Mills took the chair at l p. m.
Hon. Charles Drury gave an eloquent
address in his usual calm style. He dweit
upon the many advantages relatively ap-
pertaining to farming, and urged upon the
farmers to have a due appreciation of their
advantages. They should think twice
before leaving the farm, though its profits
are small, for other lines that are more
precarious. The college was intended to
assist the young men of the farm in doing
the work of the same on a higher plan, that
would ultimately increase the profits and
render lighter its laborious labors.
Mr. Shaw, Secretary of the Central
Farmers' Institute, spoke on the subject of
agricultural education. He aid the
farmers had & right to expect of the
Guelph College that it would turn out a
better class of farmers than if they had not
gone there. On the principle that a tree is
judged by its fruits, they were justified in
looking for & class of farming in advance of
the best methods as practised by in-
viduals, anu should look for stock kept in
such a way that they can learn useful
lessons from its management. The experiments-
ments, too, should be absolutely reliable.
On the other hand, the college had a right
to expect that the farmers. would send their
sons there as students. They should not
be expected to turn out bricks without
straw. At present not one farmer in
five thousand is patronizing the college,
though it is admirably equipped and well
managed.

assault and robbery took place near the
Woodbine. A street car driver named
Skinner had just changed his horses at the
terminus when two men entered his car,
knocked him down, kicked him and robbed
him of his money-about $4 or $5 in silver,
and bills and tickets to the amount of $10.
The miscreants got away, leaving the poor
fellow in a pitiable plight. He was barely
able to take his car to the east end stables
and report the robbery.

The Honey Crop.
Mr. C. Hellens, veteran bee keeper,
informs us that the prospects for the honey
supply this year are bad. The long spring
drought cut off the supplies from the bees,
and unless the basswood or linden trees
come out well honey will be scarce.-St.

At a. citizen meeting held in St. Cathar-
ines on Thursday evening the following
resolution was passed : :
" Whereas, the Government of Canada
has hitherto recognized the sanctity of the
Lord's Day in closing the Welland Canal
and thus allowing the employees & day of
rest, which is the inalienable right of the
British subject and a Christian man, and
secured to him by the constitution of our
country; and whereas the Government,
by recent order, has compelled the men to
remain at their posts & portion of the
Lord's Day, thereby violating the Sabbath
law of the land, and setting a bad example
to others, and at the same time robbing
the men of their right to a day of rest and
the privileges of a Christian country; be
it resolved, that this meeting enter its
solemn protest this legalized Sab.
bath-breaking, and entreat the Government
to revoke this order, and thug render
sacred its obligations to its own constitution.-
tion."
The resolution was adopted without
opposition, with hearty applause.
It was resolved that copies of this resolved.
resolution be sent to the Dominion Govern-
ment and to the press. The meeting also
passed a resolution against Sunday traffic
generally.
A Warning to the Meddlesome.
An amusing incident occurred on the
express from the Bridge at Ingersoll, on
Wednesday morning. On the train was a
young domestic, and at the station she had
occasion to leave her seat for & few
moments. She left a parasol behind her
on the seat, and a lady sitting opposite be-
came greatly exercised at what she pre-.
sumed would be the girl's loss, and picking
it up handed it to another party on the
platform, mistaking her for the owner.
When the latter returned the train was in
motion, and she learned that her sunshade
was left at, Ingersoll, and a wordy war
ensued, the would-be benefactor having
finally to pay for the parasol.
He Was Afraid to Tell.
The body of & little boy, whose parents
reside at Niagara Falls, Ont., wag found in
the river below the whirlpool on Wednes.
day. He had been swimming with a com-
panion above the whirlpool and was car-
ried out by the current. His comrade, a
very little boy, was frightened and told
nothing about the accident until the body
was reoovered on Wednesday.

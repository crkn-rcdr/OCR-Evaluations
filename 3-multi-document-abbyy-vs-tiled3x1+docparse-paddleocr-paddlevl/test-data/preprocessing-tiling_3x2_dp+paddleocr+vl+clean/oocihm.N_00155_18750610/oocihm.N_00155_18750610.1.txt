JUNE.- Ovid, in his Fasti, makes Juno
claim the honor of giving a name to this
month ; but there had been ample time be-
fore his day for an obscurity to invest the
origin of the term, and he lived before it was
the custom to investigate such matters critically-
cally. Standing as the fourth month in the
Roman calendar, it was in reality dedicated
a Jumoribus-that is, to the junior or in-
rior branch of the original legislature of
Rome, as May was a majoribus, or to the
supreme branch. Romulus'as assigned to this
month a complement of thirty days, though
in the old Latin or Alban calendar, it can-
sisted of twenty-six only. Numa deprived it
of one day, which was restored by Julius
Cresar; since which time it has remained un-
disturbed.

THE SEASON AT NIAGARA FALLS.- We LEARN
from the new paper the Register that the
season at the Falls, as at all watering places,
is unusually late this year. The weather, of
course, has a great deal to do with the num-
ber of arrivals, yet the dullness of business
throughout the country is also not without its
due importance in the matter. The extreme
cold in the early days of May prevented many
from leaving the cities, while the chilliness of
even the latter days of the month has made
the season for tourists at least two week's
later than usual. By the first days of June
Niagara Falls is usually besieged by the ad-
vance guard of that great army of sight
seers, who, later in summer, crowd every
hotel, and awaken the sleepy town to an
amazing show of life and activity. The large
hotels have all opened for the season, but the
roomy coaches roll by, after the arrival of
each train, with a scant number of passengers-
gers. Some of our shrewdest and most ex-
perienced hotel proprietors predict a short
season this year, but human foresight in
such a case as this is almost wholly at fault,
since the unknown quantities are too numerous-
ous for the solution of the problem. The
proprietors of several of our leading hotels
have put down their rates one dollar per day
belong the charges fixed at Saratoga-a and
Long Branch; and we are not at all doubtful
of their ability to furnish equal accommodations-
tions at such rates.
THE RECOVERY OF SUNKEN TREASURE.- THE
steamship'Di Dido, belonging to the London and
Edinburgh Shipping Company, left Leith for
Vigo on May 11, with a number of divers on
board. The vessel has been chartered by a
London salvage company for the purpose of
carrying on operations to recover treasure
sunk in the harbor at Vigo in the form of
specie and plate valued at several millions.
The treasure was lost in 1702, when a rich
fleet of Spanish galleons, with a convoy of
French ships of the line, were attacked bythe
combined English and Dutch fleets in Vigo
harbor. The French were beaten, and the
galleons fell into the hands The conquerors,
with the exception of six, which were set on
fire and sunk by the Spaniards to save them
from being captured. More than one at-
tempt has already been made to recover the
treasure, but these have failed from the want,
it is stated, of proper appliances. In the
event of the treasere being recovered
royalty of forty-three and a half per cent will.
be paid to the Spanish Government.

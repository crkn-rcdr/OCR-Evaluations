Thomas McAllister falls off the Schooner
“Starling " at Port Dalhousie
We learn from the St. Catharines Journal
that on Sunday last & melancholy accident
happened at Port Dalhousie, whereby a young
man named Thomas McAllister belonging to
this city, lost his life. While the schooner
" Starling " was lying at the pier the man, who
was engaged on the boat, took a seat on
the railing of the vessel, where he fell
asleep. During his slumbers he accidentally
fell overboard and was drowned. He was
seen to fall by two parties who were in a tug
boat close by. They went to his assistance, to
to pull him out when he should come
to the surface of the water, but they
could not find him The man must have
struck his head against some hard substance
in falling. The body was recovered soon
afterwards, bat life was extinct. An inquest-
was held yesterday.
Result of the Election Petition Trials.
HOW THE COMBATANTS STAND.
According to the record, eighteen Re-
formers were unseated. Had they remained
so, the results would have been undoubtedly
weakening to the Liberal party. But we find
that out of the eighteen thus unseated, the
following have been re-elected or chosen in
the Reform interest in place of the unseated
ones:
McGregor, Essex,
Norris, Lincoln.
Shibley, Addington.
Biggar, East Northumberland.
Ker, West Northumberland.
McDougall, Renfrew.
Macdonald, Cornwall.
Cook, Simcoe.
Macdonald, Centre Toronto.
Higinbotham, North Wellington.
Irving, Hamilton.
Wood, Hamilton.
And one seat has been won from the Con-
servatives:
Murray, North Renfrew.
Of the remaining ones Norfolk is lost to the
Liberals, but is again in the Election Court.
London - has gone Conservative; Huron has
returned an independent member; East To-
onto has gone Co: Conservative; North Victoria
is decided to have been Conservative from
the first, and North York has yet to be de-
fried. On the other hand the Conservatives
have retained Kingston, Niagara, Middlesex
and Leeds; have lost Renfrew, and Monck is
yet to be decided. The total result of the
elections that have been held for the Do.
minion Commons as the result of the election
trials is, that the Liberals have lost three
constituencies and gained one-leaving a net
profit to the Conservatives of two, or four
votes on a division. As the Liberal majority
in the House ranges somewhere between
fifty and a hundred, there can hardly be
sufficient ground for a panic in the fact that
the Opposition ranks number two more, and
the Government sile two less than they did
a year ago.
In the local election cases the results are
that the following seats have been vacated:

To the Opposition losses should also be
added South Ontario, as Jud Wilson has
practically decided that the decision first
given in that case ought to be reversed.
That makes in all four Reform and three
Opposition members gone, the new elections
in all those cases being yet to be held.
The results of the isolated Dominion held-
tions held and the character of the constitu-
encies vacated justify us in expecting not.
only that of the seven new members at least
the only proportions will be preserved, but
that there will be a change for the better.
There are good reasons for hoping that both
North Wentworth and South Ontario will
reverse their late decision and return men of.
the same political faith as they were return-
represented by the last Legislature.-Liberal.

NIAGARA, June 6th.- Instruction of the
various corps in drill was progressing actively.
yesterday, their being the daily parades. The
period of annual drill being now limited to
twelve days, the officers commanding the
camp, no doubt, thought the granting of a
half-holiday would too seriously interfere
with the accomplishment of tho object for
which the troops are assembled, and so hard
work had to be stuck to. The infantry are-
ments were all out on the common,. several
of them being exercised in battalion movements-

that shows how well up the companies
officers must be in their duty. The cavalry
during the afternoon were formed in two
squadrons and practised marching past before
Lieut.- Col. Durie, D. A. G., who was attended
by Major Moore, of the Brigade staff. The
artillery were also out for a while and per-
formed various field ld manœuvres.
There was a large and brilliant party at the
Queen's Royal Hotel, last night in honor of
the Brigade in camp. The hotel, now ele-
gantly fitted up, was thrown open to the
guests, who enjoyed an evening of gaiety.

Thomson and daughters, Queenston; Mrs.
Wilson, Miss Eccles and Miss Lawder, St.
Catharines; Mrs. H. Arthurs and the Misses

Hobbs, U.S.A.; Lieut. Tiernon, U. S. A.;.;
and Dr. Woodruff, U. S. A. The guests spent

leaving. acknowledged the hospitality they had
received. The band of the 13th Battalion

SPRING IMPORTATIONS.
The Alert and the Discovery, the two ships
of the English Polar expedition, sailed Satur-
day. This is, all odds, the most thorough-
ly equipped expedition that has ever ever-
trated the Artic Sea, and it will also have the
benefit of the experience of the many Arctic
voyages that have been made by American,
Swedish, German, and Austrian expeditions
during the last twenty-five years. Knowing,
as we do, by the voyage of the Polaris, that a
vessel can penetrate Smith's Sound to at least
the parallel of 80 degrees, we have a right to
expect that the Alertandtheel the Discovery, either
of which vessel is far superior in steam-power
to Hall's wretched little steamer, can reach & a
much higher latitude by the same route.
That this will be done, and that the Pole will
be reached by sledging parties starting from
the point at which the progress of the ships
is finally arrested, we have good reason to
hope; and certainly the brave men of the
English expedition will have the best wishes
of.the whole civilized world for their success.
An Englishman has just accomplished the
ascent of Mount Blanc at a time of the year
when no tourist had ever ventured on the at-
tempt. Mr. Kennedy, member of the English
Alpine Club, set out from Chamonix on May
15, accompanied by two guides, and reached
Grands-Mulets at four in the afternoon. The
following day the party started at three in the
morning for the summit, and after contending
with the difficulties of the snow giving way
under their feet, and of a burning sun, they
arrived at the Grand Plateau. From that
point the surface was firmer, so that they
were enabled to attain the highest point to-
ward noon; and there the travellers planted an
alpenstock and drank a bottle of champagne
"in honor of the tourist world, past, present,
and to come. Owing to the intense heat the
descent was difficult, but it was safely effected
at eight in the evening.

A trip through these townships has shown
that the fall wheat here is a great failure, and

A SPUR II THE HEAD.
The old proverb has it that a spur in the head
worth two in the heel, but a similar heel-
twrience to that of the writer would render this
assertion problematical. In hoisting an old
hen from her nest beneath the roost, an-
fronticere, affected by her loud complaints, and
inspired by true gallantry, descended like an
eagle on his prey, upon. the occiput of the in-
truder, driving his spurs through a felt hat
and the scalp of his antagonist, much to the
direcomfort and vexation of the latter, who, in
admiration, however, of the gallant act, re-
versed a hasty decision to convert him into
pot-pie, into a flattering compliment to his
esprit du corps, though without any desire for
a repeated exhibition cf it on his own cra-
nium.

WHAT HANGING IS LIKE.
The writer from what he has seen would
counsel any disposed to try voluntary hang-
ing to make a thorough job of it, for any-
thing short of it is worse in its agonies than a
dozen deaths. The following case will fully
warrant this assertion. The wretched man
being cut down before fatal strangulation had
taken place, for two hours or so shrieked for
breath as if choking to death, the most shock-
ing spectacle that could be witnessed or
thought of. His wild and suffering aspect,
his frantic ravings, combined with the
thoughts of his being a suicide, along with
the painful struggles of resuscitation, formed
a scene which must be seen to be fully appre-
ciated.

FIELD DAY — VISIT OF THE MINISTER OF MILITIA.
NIAGARA, June 7.— This has been quite a
red letter day in the experience of the
volunteers here. The usual drill took place
at 6.30 a. m., but the second parade was o--
dered three-quarters of an hour earlier
than usual, namely at 9.45, when the whole
brigade marched out to the common and
formed under the superintendence of Col.
Villiers, Brigade Major, in a line of con-
tiguous quarter columns, with the cavalry
and artillery on the right. Shortly after-
wards the various corps deployed into line.
The Hon. Mr. Vail, Minister of Militia,
drove upon the ground in an open carriage,
attended by an escort from the 2nd Regi-
ment of cavalry, and accompanied by Lieut:--
Col. Durie, D. A. G., and Col. Denison, Super-
intendent of Drill, and other. ofi Sucers. He
then drove slowly down. the front of the line
and up the rear, the bands of the various bat-
tallions playing as he passed them. The
troops then marched passed at a walk, the
infantry in column. The next movement
was a march past of the cavalry and artillery
at the trot, and of the infantry in quarter
column. The music was furnished by the
united bands brigaded for the occasion. After
this the force reformed line of quarter
columns, again deployed into line, advanced
in line, and then retired by fours from the
right of companies. The brigade was then
formed into three es. of a hollow square, and
the Minister of Militia drove into the centre.
He made a speech expressive of the satisfac-
faction his visit had afforded him, promising
that he would oppose to his uttermost any
proposed alienation of the ordnance property
at Niagara, so suitable for. military purposes,
and that the Government: nt would be solicitous
for the interests of the volunteers of the Do-
minion. The troops, led by Colonel Denison,
gave three cheers for the Queen, and then re-
turned to Camp Having been under arms
for four hours, the usual afternoon parade
was in many corps considerately dispensed
with. This, the first field day, may be con-
sidered a great success, and Colonel Durie ex-
pressed his complete satisfaction with the be-
havior of the men. The heavy rain of the
previous night had laid the dust and freshened
vegetation, but the sun shone bright and hot.
A few ladies and other visitors occupied
the ramports of the old fortifications,
and there were a good many carriages
on the grounds. The general opinion ex-
pressed is that the various corps exhibited a
wonderful degree of proficiency considering
the short time they have been under training,
not a full week yet.
In the course of the afternoon the Minis-
ter of Militia held a levee at the Queen's
Royal Hotel, at which a great many of the
officers in camp attended.
The Brigade Staff Sergeants give a dinner
to their friends in camp to-morrow evening.
It is believed there will be brigade drill
every day this week until the inspection by
the Major-General takes place.
Excellent order prevails in camp and in
the town; and discipline, which is rigidly
enforced, is everywhere cheerfully submitted
to.
The inspection by tho Major-General will
probably take place on Wednesday.- Maills

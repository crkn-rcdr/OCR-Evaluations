The city has bought a lot of land
66 feet wide and 270 feet in length
extending from the I. C. R. tracks
to University avenue, from Mr. Geo.
W. Babbitt, manager of the Bank of
Nova Scotia at St. Andrews and a
former Fredericton man, for the pur-
pose of extending Saunders street.
The price paid is to be $1,400 and
the purchase will probably be con-
cluded at an early date.

It may be some time, however, be-
fore the extension of Saunders street
can be carried on, however, because
a letter from Mr. F. P. Brady, general
superintendent of the I. C. R., which
was read last evening, said that the
lay out of the Intercolonial's facili-
ties in this city had not yet been
determined.

A Canoe Trip.

Mr. James M. Lemont and Mr.
Frank Harrison, musical instructor
and organise, left this morning for
Woodstock, where they will meet
Mr. Wilmot Lemont, now of Chicago,
and form a canoeing party, with
Frank Noel as guide. They will tour
down the St. John river to this city,
taking in the interesting tributaries
of the river so far as navigable en
route. The party epxect to return,
home in about a week.

ferred to the light committee.

At the Gaiety.

at the Gaiety last evening, "King of

Steamboat Service for Jemseg.

The steamer Hampstead commenc-
ed today on an extended route which
promises to bring a new district in
closer touch with Fredericton and
thus bring business to this city which
has heretofore gone elsewhere. The
Hampstead started its trips today
from Lower Jemseg, instead of Gage-
town, and was thus about an hour
later than usual in arriving here, but
brought a large number of passen-
gers from the Jemseg district. It is
proposed to make the Jemseg trip on
Tuesdays and Fridays during the bal-
ance of the season, thus serving the
additional territory as well as the
Gagetown ser ‘ce.

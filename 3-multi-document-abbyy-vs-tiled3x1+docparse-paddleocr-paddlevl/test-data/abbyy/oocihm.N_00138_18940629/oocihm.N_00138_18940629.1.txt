LOWE-BUTLER.

A very pretty wedding took place yes-
terday afternoon at the residence of Mrs.
Butler, Brantford, being the marriage
of her only, daughter, Stella, to Mr.
George Lowe, Hamilton, Ont. The bride
was attired in a rich brown traveling
dress and was unattended. The ceremony
was performed by Rev. D. Hutchinson,
pastor of Park Baptist Church. The
bride was the recipient of a number of
handsome presents, among which may
be mentioned a silver water pitcher and
server from the choir of Park Church,
of which she has been a leading mem-
ber for a number of years, also of a
handsome Bible from the teachers and
officers of the Eagle Place Baptist mis-
sion, where she was a teacher. The bride
is well and favorably known in this
city, while Mr. Lowe is the senior mem-
ber of the firm of Lowe & Farrel, elec-
trical supply merchants, Hamilton. The
happy couple left on the 4 o’clock train
for eastern points, after which they
will reside at No. 152 Market street,
Hamilton. They carry with them the
best wishes of a large circle of friends.—
Expositor.

IN HYMEN’S BONDS.

A most enjoyable event took place at
the residence of Mr. and Mrs. Geo.
Towers, Locke street north, last evening,
it being the marriage of their daughter
Annie to Mr.Edward Stove. Rev. J. G.
Shearer, in whose church the bride was
a highly esteemed worker, performed
the ceremony. Miss Alice Towers, sister
of the bride, acted as bridesmaid, and
the bridegroom was supported by Mr.
Robert Taylor, of Niagara-on-the-Lake.
The bride looked very attractive, dressed
in cream, carrying a magnificent bouquet
of roses. The bridesmaid was also at-
tired in cream, and carried a bouquet of
sweet peas. The ceremony over, the
guests sat down to a sumptuous supper,
after which a pleasant evening was
spent. The large number of handsome
and costly presents received by the bride
testified to her popularity among her
many friends and acquaintances, who
wish both her and her husband every
happiness.

A STORY FROM LONDON.

It is About Lady Aberdeen and Comes by
Way of New York.

Lady Aberdeen, so it is said, had her-
self driven to a music store in London,
where she selected the latest composi-
tions- Cowen, Leoncavallo’s ** Berceuse,
Sullivan’s symphonies, in fact all the
pieces en vogue at the present time. The
handsome lady appeared to have fin-
wished her purchases. As she laid a gold
piece on the counter: " Oh, I beg,
she said to the music clerk, whose heart
was fluttering under the glimmer of her
pretty eyes: " Oh ! I beg let me have
• One kiss before we part." "Wha—wha
—what ?" faltered the clerk, who ex-
pected anything but that. *‘One kiss
before we part,’ repeated the pretty pure
chaser in a most winning tone of voice.

Could he withstand this seeming plea!
No! Casting a furtive glance about
the shop, he closed his eyes, shot out his
arms, drew the elegant head of the hand-
some woman toward him and pressed
a luscious kiss on her lips. A cry, a
sound like a slap on the cheek, a com-
motion, a scene. The clerk was dis-
missed on the spot ; no excuses were ac-
cepted. In the court followed a sequel
—the Countess complained on account of
the kiss, which gave her great offence-
the clerk sued for damages for the loss of
his position.

The first case is decided against him
and his own suit dismissed. At an ap-

peal he won the first case, as it was
held that his action toward Lady Aber-
deen was the result of her irresistible
charms. His complaint, however, was
dismissed by the gallant judge, . who
remarked that the kiss was a sufficient
remuneration for all the losses he may
have met with. Both parties were
satisfied with the verdict. Lady Aber-
deen is said to even feel proud of it. As
to the clerk--hm, he is to-day the most
popular clerk in the British Empire,

Hamilton's Crying Need for Another
Newspaper to be Supplied.m

It is currently reported that Mr. W.
W. Buchanan, who advised the prohibi-
tionists of West Hamilton to stay at
home on election day, has a new project
on hand, no less than the establishment
of another daily paper in Hamilton. He
has six promises of $1,000 to start with,
and his scheme includes the erection of
a handsome building. History repeats it-
self. Another temperance daily called
the Tribune was started in Hamilton
once upon a time. Its owners also began
by erecting a building. The paper soon
died from an overdose of bosses, and the
common stockholders lost their invest-
ment. People who have money to waste,
now that times have again become
flush, will hand it over to W. W. B. and
let him show them how soon he can dis-
pose of it.

DOMINION DAY CRICKET.

The Galt Cricket Club has sent word
that it has been unable to get up a
team to meet Hamilton on Monday next,
so that the Hamilton Cricket Club will
have only one match on. That will be
with the London team, and will be played
in this city.

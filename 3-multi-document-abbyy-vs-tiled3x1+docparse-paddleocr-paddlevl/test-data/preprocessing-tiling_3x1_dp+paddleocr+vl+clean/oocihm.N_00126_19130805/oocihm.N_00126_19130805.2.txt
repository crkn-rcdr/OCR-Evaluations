Welsford, Aug. 4—The Better Farm-
ing Coaches arrived here today, and
in spite of the heavy rain a large num-
ber of farmers from Welsford and the
surrounding districts visited the train,
some driving in sixteen miles or more.
All showed great interest in the edu-
cational features and exhibits, and
kept the officials accompanying the
the
train busy answering questions, and
explaining various phases of the work
being carried the Farming Spe-
cial.
After inspecting the exhibits on the
train the farmers went to Victoria
Hall, where the Women's Institute
of Welsford served luncheon, and
made the gathering feel at home.
After S. L Peters delivered a lecture
1
on fruit growing; H. P. Duross spoke
/
on soil drainage and bee culture; C.
W.McDougall spoke on dairying:;
Andrew Elliott discussed soil culture
and S. Forsyth gave a talk on seed
selection.
Rev. J. S. Greig officiated as chairman-
man of the meeting, and at the con-
clusion of the lectures H. W. Woos,
M. L. A., moved a vote of thanks to
the speakers for their interesting and
instructive talks, and to the Provin-
cial Government and C.P.R.for send-
ing out the Farming Coaches.
This
was seconded by W. S. Harding and
enthusiastically.
" God Save the King " was then sung
and the farmers, after an interesting
and profitable day, departed to their
homes.

SUNSTROKE.
Anyone overcome by the heat should
be immediately removed to the nearest
shade, and the collar of shirt or dress
should be loosened.
Send immediately
for the nearest physician, and give the
person cool drinks of water, black tou
or coffee, if able to swallow.
If the skin is hot and dry, place the
person in a sitting position against a
tree, wall, or anything that will be a
support to the back; sponge with or pour
some cold water over the body and
limbs, and apply to the head pounded
ice wrapped in a towel or other cloth.
If there be uo ice at hand, keep a cold
cloth on the head and pour cold water
on it as well as on the body.
lf the person is pale, very faint and
pu se-feeble, lay him down on the back,
let him inhale ammonia for a few see-
seconds, or give him a teaspoonful of aro-
matie spirits of ammonia or tincture
of ginger in two tablespoonfuls of wa-
ter. Use no cold water upon the head
or body, but rub the hands and feet and
apply warm applications to the same

A TERRIBLE ACCIDENT.
Yesterday morning about nine o'clock
Richard Langlois, a married man, aged
50, met with a terrible accident at
Fletcher's planing mills, Yonge street,
Toronto. Langlois was putting a bolt
on a pulley, when by some means his
left arm became caught in the machin
ery. Before the engine could be stopped
he was dra wn up to the ceiling, and
his left arim crushed almost to a pulp
between the pulley and the beams. His
left thigh was also badly crushed. The
unfortunate man was removed in the
ambulance to St. Michael's hospital,
where the injured arm was amputated.
At an early hour this morning Langlois
was reported as doing well.

A COLD MEAT MINCE.
Take 1 lb. of lean cooked meat, and
chop it up very finely: soak a thick slice
of bread in milk, and press the milk quite
ont when soaked: beat it out with a
fork; add it to the eat, dnd mix all
well together; add pepper and salt to
taste: now beat up ono egg, and mix
it with the meat and bread. Have a
nice deep pudding dish, and butter it
well inside: ornament the bottom and
sides of dish with hard-boiled eggs cut
and placed neatly; pour the mixture in
and bake one hour; turn out gently.
Make a sauce thus: Put ou the bones
of the meat, broken up, to boil one or
2 hours, with an onion and a piece of
carrot and turnip; fry an onion brown
im 1-2 oz. butter or dripping: when very
brown, sprinkle in 1 dessert-spoonful
of flour, and stir till the flour iswet;
now add 1-2 pint of the stock, 1 table-
spoonful catsup, a little pepper and salt
to taste; stir till if boils-it should not
be too thick; strain this sauce round
your meat, and serve hot.

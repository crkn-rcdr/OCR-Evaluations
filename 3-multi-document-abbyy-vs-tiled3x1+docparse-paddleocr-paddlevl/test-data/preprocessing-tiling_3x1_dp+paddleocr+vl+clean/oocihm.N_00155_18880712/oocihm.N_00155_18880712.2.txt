A last (Wednesday) night's Niagara Falls
despatch says: True to his announcement,
Robert William Flack, of Syracuse, N.Y.,.,
with his life-life-saving boat, the Phantom, at.
tempted to shoot the Whirlpool rapids this
afternoon, and his foolhardy trip cost him
his life. Flack arrived with his little craft
at Suspension Bridge, N.Y., on Wednesday,
June 27th, and made the New York Central
Hotel his headquarters. He has been busy.-
ing himself taking observations of the river
and distributing photographs of his boat
with himself seated in it, attached to
which are subscription lists, which were
placed in all public places on both sides of
the river for voluntary subscriptions in
order to defray the expenses of his
trip here and making other preparations
for his perilous trip. When in conversation
with him yesterday, and asked if he fully
understood the river, he replied yes, and
said he had often been in rougher seas
than the Whirlpool rapids. He said his
object was to prove the usefulness of his
craft as & life-saving boat and an invention
of his own on which he expected to get &
patent. The craft would seat about five
persons. At the bottom were air chambers
filled with some composition of his own in-
vention. It was propelled by means of
small paddles worked by a crank in the
boat. In the seating apartment there was
seat to which he strapped himself, and he
aid he used no ballast of any kind to keep
his craft down. When asked if he was not
afraid that when his boat came in contact
with the large breakers it would turn up.
side down, and the weight of his body
would keep it so and drown him, he said no;
that it would not remain so if it did turn
over.
Flack was an inventor and carpen-
penter, and constructed the Phantom dur-
ing his spare moments last winter.
lived for twelve years in Toronto. For
four years his home has been in Syracuse,
where h has a wife and five children.
He was alive when near the spot where
Capt. Webb ran his head against & rock,
but Flack died by strangulation. When
near the whirlpool, after his boat had sev-
eral times been capsized, he responded
with a wave of the hand to cheers from the
crowd on the top of the banks.

Painful Accident to Mr. Trow, M. P.
The publicw will be sorry to learn that a
painful accident occurred to Mr. Trow,
M. P. on Monday. Anxious to spend part
of the holiday with his friend in Mitchell,
he arrived in the town by the noon train,
and after dinner was induced to visit the
driving park, where horse races were in
progress. In the park he was invited to a
seat on the judges, and while chatting-
seatting with those around him the plank on
which he and others were sitting slipped
off its support and in falling Mr. Trow's
leg was caught under it and severely
bruised.-Stratford Beacon.

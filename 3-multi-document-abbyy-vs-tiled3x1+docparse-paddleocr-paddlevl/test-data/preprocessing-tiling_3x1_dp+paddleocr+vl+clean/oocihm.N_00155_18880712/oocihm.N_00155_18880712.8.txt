THE CHARGES AGAINST PARNELL.
He Wishes a Committee of the House of
Commons to Investigate...
A London cable says: In the House of
Commons yesterday Sir Wilfred Lawson,
Home Ruler, asked whether the Govern.-
ment would grant the appointment oi a
committee of the House to inquire into the
charges against the leaders of the National
party in the House of Commons. W.H.
Smith, the Government leader, said the
House was incompetent to inquire into the
charges. A proper inquiry could be ob.
obtained through the ordinary tribunals. Mr.
Parnell later gave notice to the House that
on Thursday he would submit a motion for
the appointment of & committee to inquire
into the authenticity of the letters pub.
published by the Times in its articles on "Par-
nellism and Crime," and those read at the
recent trial of the suit of O'Donnell against
the Times, in which he and other Nationalist
members were charged with serious crimes.
He would also ask the Government to ap.-
point a day to discuss the subject and give
him an opportunity to repel the foul and
unfounded charges made against him by
Attorney-General Webster in the trial of
the suit of O'Donnell against the Times.
Mr. Parnell's remarks were greeted with
loud cheers from the Irish members.

That Earthquake.
KINGsToN, July 9.-The shock of earth.-
quake last night at ll p. m. was felt in this
whole district.
Along the line of the Grand Trunk the
shock wag distinctly felt and unmistakeable.
At Napanee the shock came first as if from
an explosion, followed by a rumbling, rat.
thing sound as the wave passed from north
to south. Nearly the whole town was
awakened.
At Enterprise and Tamworth the shock
was so violent that goods were knocked out
of shelves in stores. The shock was felt in
Belleville, Picton and Deseronto. It seemed
as if the wave travelled directly from north
to south. The shock wag plainly felt at
North Port, Prince Edward County, aud
the residents thought there had been an ex-
plosion. The houses trembled and the people
were much excited.

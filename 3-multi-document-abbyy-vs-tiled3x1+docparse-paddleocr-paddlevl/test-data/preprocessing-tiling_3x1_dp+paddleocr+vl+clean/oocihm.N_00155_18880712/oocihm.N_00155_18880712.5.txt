A SATURDAY NIGHT PERFORMANCE.
A. Pair of Mountaineers Make Things
Lively on John Street.
Saturday evening on the Court House
square, John street south, & pretty lively
skirmish took place between & pair of
mountaineers known as James McAulay,
his wife Kate and the police officer on duty.
It appears McAulay came to the city in the
early part of the afternoon to have some
fun. He commenced his game by visiting
all the saloons on the street in rotation to
test the quality of their stock, and became
in a short time to feel very jolly. As Jim
is considerable of & step dancer, he made
things pretty lively for the young urchins
in the neighborhood. All he required
was & monkey to have coppers in piles.
His partner struck town later in the after-
noon to hunt her lord and master. After
diving into the different drinking places,
she succeeded in capturing him at the cor-
ner of John and Jackson streets. While
they were discussing matters in & lively
manner a policeman appeared on the scene
and advised Jim to shake the city dust off
his shoes and retreat up the mountain
again as Boon as po ε esible. But brave Jim
could not be bluffed so easily. He com-
menced to show up the pedigree of the
officer in the choicest language when he was
immediately arrested and resisted with all
his might. It became necessary to lay Jim
on the grass and apply the handcuffs. In
the meantime Kate started up a howl worse
than an Indian war whoop, and in less than
five minutes about two hundred people
gathered to see who was murdered. With
great difficulty the prisoner was forced
through the crowd with Kate in the rear
keeping up her music. Jim was safely
locked up, and the magistrate being pre-
sent thought it was best to give Kate a bath
also until her head would get a little
settled. This morning the magistrate
made James cost him $4 or 30 days
in jail, while Kate got off for half that
amount.

It seems as if the treatment which emi-
grants have received this spring in Toronto
is likely to do more good in checking the
operations of the spurious philanthropists
in England than all the warnings and pro-
tests of labor organizations. One of the
most mischievously active of the organiza-
tions which have been formed for curing
English pauperism by exporting the paupers
is the Self-Help Emigration Society, of
which a Rev. Mr. Mackay is Secretary.
Many of the poor immigrants who were
dumped down penniless and friendless at
the immigrant sheds this spring have to
thank this gang of self-self-styled philanthropists.
pists for the sad plight in which they found
themselves. The sights seen by those who
visited the sheds at the time will not soon
be forgotten. Men, women and children
huddled into an evil-smelling den in which
no farmer would house cattle which he
valued; benumbed with cold and well
nigh starving while the Dominion and
Provincial authorities tied and untied,
ravelled and unravelled red tape to decide
whose duty it was to feed them. Indeed,
had it not been for the passive charity of
citizens and for the active charity of the
Salvation Army lasses, there would have
been a worse tale to tell. It is gratifying
to see that some of the unfortunate ones
are sending letters home, which are being
published in papers which the working
classes read, exposing the fraud by which
they were cheated into coming to a country
where the labor market is already over-
crowded. In this way it is to be hoped
that the trap-setting and snare-laying of
the bogus philanthropists may be made

Brown's Twenty-Three Wives.
The Detroit man Brown, who is under
arrest for being & bigamist 22 times over,
and who confesses to having married 17 of
the women complaining against him, had
an ingenious
method of procuring his
" helpmeets.
He advertised for a house-
keeper. Young women applied to him for
B situation. Selecting the one which best
suited his fancy he would make flattering
offers to her. Oftentimes the young
women objected to living with a widower
and they wanted time to think it over. His
last wife wan given three weeks. In the
meantime he called to see her, and the last
time he came made a proposition for mar-
riage. There would be no disgrace then ;
besides, he, owned a big farm in the in-
terior of the State, had lots of money, and
after a honeymoon of two or three weeks
they would go to the farm. After the
" honeymoon, however, he invariably set
his trap for other women, would leave his
newly made wife and take unto his bosom
another. The old man, the News affirms,
has grown white the past few days, and at
times is unable to sit up.

Grimsby Camp Guessing.
Mr. Noah Phelps, President of the
Grimsby Camp Ground Company, offered
& prize of 25 cents to any child on the
ground who would figure out correctly the
number of nails used in building the roof
of the Pantheon. Answers were handed in
on Monday, and the President was forced
to close the doors at 12 noon or he would
have been bankrupt. The solutions came
in more rapidly than he expected, and the
children will have plenty of spending money
for the rest of this week. There are 690, 000
nails in the 200, 000 feet of lumber used in
constructing the dome.
An Ottawa Sensation.
An Ottawa correspondent telegraphs: :
The sensation of the day, so far as Ottawa
is concerned, is the resignation of nearly all
the officers of the Governor. General's Foot
Guards. For some time past Lieut.-Col.l.
Pennington Macpherson and his officers
have not pulled well together. It is alleged
by the officers that Col. Macpherson real-
izes too fully the importance of his position,
ignoring altogether the advice of his staff,
and this has led to a coldness springing up
which has culminated in a batch of sixteen
resignations being sent'in.

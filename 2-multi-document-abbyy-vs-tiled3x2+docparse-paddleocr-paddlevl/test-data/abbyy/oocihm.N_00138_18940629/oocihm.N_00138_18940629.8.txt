THE DEATH RATTLE.

The P. P. A. Fragments Likely Soon to be
Gathered to the Father of Evil.

The P. P. A’s. or Amoreans held an ex-
perience meeting at the north end ren-
dezvous last night, the sad-looking
couples and trios discussing the result
of Tuesday's electionin grumbling
undertones as they shuffled down East
avenue toward their Council chamber.
The meeting was pretty well attended
and there was much mutual recrimina-
tion and many accusations of unfaith-
fulness. Each of the members seemed
to view the others with suspicion and
complaints were made that there was
not enough secrecy observed and that
some of the boycotters were themselves
suffering because of it becoming known
that they were in the ring. One bar-
ber asserted that he had lost a great
deal of custom and had bet some hard
earned money and lost it. Some of the
more rattle-pated talked big about
what could be done if a protest were
entered, while others said they had paid
their last dollar into the concern. There
was a strong section of the meeting
inclined to be rebellious and resent
being used longer as cat's paws for
the place-hunters who run it. Alto-
gether the meeting was not of the most
harmonious character and argued the ra-
pid decadence of the most abominable
fungoid excrescence that has ever threa-
tened the Canadian social structure.

" Holiday House" at the Beach will
be ready to receive boarders on Monday
next. The house was formally opened by
Bishop Hamilton yesterday afternoon
in the presence of about 40 ladies inter-
terested in the work of the Girls'
Friendly Society. It is situated less
than ten minutes' walk southerly from
the Elsinore Depot and is a neat, plain
frame structure facing the lake. A ver-
andah runs along the whole front and
is to be continued on one side. It is a
typical Beach building, light, airy and
cool. On the ground floor is a large liv-
ing room, with four neat bed rooms off
it, and a convenient kitchen. The up-
stairs is one large sleeping room fur-
nished with fifteen single cots, arranged
around three sides and separated from
the main room by curtains. All the con-
veniences necessary for a well-equip-
ped ladies' summer holiday resort are
provided. On the bay side, in the midst
of; a lovely grove, is the boat and bath-
ing house.

The opening ceremonies yesterday were
very interesting. Bishop Hamilton was
assisted by Rev. Thomas Geoghegan.
During the service the Bishop made a
few appropriate remarks.

After the service refreshments were
partaken of.

Among those to whom the ladies were
indebted for assistance in various ways
are Messrs. Geo. S. Papps, Stanley Mills
& Co., A. McKeown, Thomas Burrows,
McCallum & Hall, Adam Clark, T. W.

MacKelcan, Mrs. Doolittle, Mrs. Wylie
and others.

Mr. Mercer built the house, and the

A Resort in One’s Own House—How to
Decorate and Furnish an Airy Apart-
ment for the Stay-at-Home Members of
the Family.

One of the great benefits of going
away in summer, writes Ella Rodman
Church in Harper’s Bazar, is the entire
change of scene and surroundings, and
the benefit remains even when the
change is not decidedly for the better.
But as all cannot leave their homes
every season, and the reasons which
conspire to make going away inexpedi-
ent often have nothing to do with lack
of means, the next best thing is to get
the needed change by transforming the
outward aspect of familiar places.

The moving furniture into different
niches and corners, with the substitu-
tion of matting for carpet, and thin
curtains in place of heavier drapery,
makes a very decided change in the ap-
pearance of any room; but when an en-
tire change of room can be afforded,
with, a different outlook, a substitution
of doors for windows and windows for
doors, walls of another tint and belong-
ings that seem new because of several
months' forgetfulness, the delightfulness
of a summer parlor is fully appreciated
by those for whom it makes their only
outing.

THE GENERAL EFFECT.

With a double house and a small
family, which not infrequently go to-
gether, the summer room is easily
achieved; and in many other cases, a
little management would render it a
possibility. The general effect of such
a room should be cool but not chilling—
it should offer- comfort in depressing
rainstorms as well as in fervent heat,
and to do this effectually an open fire-
place is one of the first requirements.
Having "glorified the room" with this
unfailing promoter of cheerfulness, we
can now proceed to the scheme of white
and gold, with variations which seems
in its natural element in the adorning
of a summer room. In a grand apart-
ment that is used chiefly in cold weath-
II. er, white and gold has a very chilling
effect, yet as a general’thing this treat-
ment is seldom found elsewhere. As
white and other light tones increase the
apparent size of a room, they are better
adapted to less space: but even here the
white should take an ivory tint.

All sorts of pretty delicate colors are
in harmony with this warm tone of
white. Old rose, like a sunset cloud; a
suggestion of pistache, that loveliest ‘of
greens; or a robin's egg blue on the
walls, with doors and other woodwork
in ivory white, touched up with dead
gold—are equally charming. If the ceil-
ings are high enough, a width of bro-
cade will make a dainty frieze.

the East End.

cord.

ERSKINE’S PROTEST.

To the Manager and Board of Direc-
tors of the Hamilton Street Railway
Company: Gentlemen,-At a large and
representative meeting of the congréga-
tion of Erskine Church, held last even-
ing. 27th inst., the following resolution
was carried by a unanimous standing
vote: This meeting deeply regrets that
the city street railway company has
seen fit to start running its cars on
Sunday : that we were all deeply pained
to hear the whirring wheels and clang-
ing bells robbing the day of the quiet
so essential to rest as well as to worship.
That we do most earnestly protest
against this unwelcome innovation and
against the plea that the church people
want it, believing, as we do, that an
overwhelming majority of church goers
were deeply grieved with the running of
the cars last Sunday, and that we ear-
nestly urge the company, in the interest
of worship, of morality, and of their
employees, to discontinue the cars at
once, assured as we are, that the run-
ning of such cars is a most serious nui-
sance to the social, moral and religious
healthfulness of our city life.

Signed on behalf of the congregation,
James Gill, M. Turnbull. C. L. Frazer,
J. R. Moodie, Robert Whyte,

LANTERN SLIDE EXCHANGE.

The regular monthly meeting of the
Camera section was held last evening,
President J. R. Moodie in the chair. The
order of business was suspended in order
to introduce Secretary A. M. Lake, of
the Toronto Camera Club, who said he
thought it advisable to extend the in-
terest and usefulness of the Canadian
clubs by co-operating with the various
photographic societies and forming a
Canadian lantern slide exchange. A
hearty vote of thanks was given him
for valuable information. In all prob-
ability. if satisfactory arrangements can
be made with the customs, Toronto and
Hamilton clubs will join the American
exchange next season. The reports of
three outings were presented, and the
secretary's anecdotes of the wildcat and
the life preserver were thoroughly ori-
ginal. Messrs. McKenzie and Lottridge
were elected members of the club.

A BIGAMIST EXPOSED.

The Northwest Mounted Police Com-
missione at Edmonton communicated
with the Dundas authorities in refer-
ence to the antecedents of Robt. Vance,
a former resident of Waterdown, who is
held there on a charge of bigamy. The
communication states that Vance mar-
ried another woman in the Northwest,
but is supposed to have a wife living in
this country. The Dundas authorities:
investigated the case, and found that
Robert Vance was married to a Miss
Eliza Clark at St. James’ Church, Dun-
das, on October 4, 1871, and that Mrs.
Vance is living in Waterdown and has
a family of grown up sons and daugh-
ters.

SPLENDID GARDEN PARTY.

Last evening a delightful garden party
was held at Mrs. Tilden’s beautiful
grounds on Main street cast, in behalf
of the Ladies’ Aid of Emerald Street
Methodist Church. The attendance was
large. A fine programme of music was
rendered by an efficient orchestra, un-
der the leadership of Mr. J. S. Hampson.
A goodly sum was realized.

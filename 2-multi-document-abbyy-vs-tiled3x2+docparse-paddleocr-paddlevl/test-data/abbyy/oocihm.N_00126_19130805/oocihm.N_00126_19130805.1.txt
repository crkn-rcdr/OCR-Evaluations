MEXICAN TROUBLE

Mexico City, Aug. 5.— How Matthew Gourd, an American
farmer, was lashed to a tree and forced to witness an attack on his
two daughters by bandits, is a story brought here today from
Tampico.

The person relating the story said the bandits first requested
a cigarette from Gourd, who complied, and a peso was offered in
payment for them. Gourd was then tied and with a knife at his
throat was ordered to produce his money. He told his seventeen-
year-old daughter to bring the money from the house, and on her
appearance she was seized and together with her sister aged thir-
teen years, was mistreated. When the rebels left the women un-
tied the father, who reported the assault to the authorities at Tam-
pico. One version of the affair is that the assailants were not rebels,
but peons employed on Gourd's own farm near the Atascador
colony.

Washington, Aug. 5.—That no an-
nouncement of policy or further
steps will be taken by President Wfl-
son in the Mexican situation until
former Governor John Lynd, of Min-
nesota, personal representative of
the President, arrives in Mexico City

Mr. Lynd carries instructions to

whether the assistance of the Unit-

be received.

the authority of President Wilson

the United States. This will involve
the resignation of Provisional Presi-
dent Huerta in favor of the previous
president selected by the Mexican

first appointed minister of foreign re-
lations in conformity with the law cf
cussession in Mexico.

tion circles today, that while Mr.
Lynd 's position now is that of adviser
to the American Embassy, he will
act in reality as an ambassador, to
be named ambassador when a stable
government is organized and recog-
nition is extended by the United
States.

Disappointment in Mexico.

Mexico City, Aug. 5—The news of
the dismissal of Henry Lane Wilson,
the American Ambassador, and the
appointment by President Wilson of
ex-Governor Lind, of Minnesota, to
come to Mexico as a special peace
emissary has caused keen disappoint-
ment and a good deal of apprehension
amongst Americans and also amongst
Mexicans here. Strong belief here
prevails that the course of action tak-
en by the United States administra-
tion is unwise, and so far from recon-
ciling opposing factions the more pro-
bable result will be to make the exist-
ing confusion worse confounded.

General Huerta has emphatically de-
clared that he will neither resign nor
accept mediation, and it is feared that
President Wilson's peace emissary
will make little or no impression upon
him until the United States makes for-
mal recognition of his government.

London, Aug 5.—The Washington
reports of Great Britain's intention to
establish a great naval base at Ber-
muda are regarded here as a gross
exaggeration and misconception of
the Admiralty’s plans relative to the
naval utiliatizon of the island.

It is admitted that the Admiralty
is taking measures to increase the
Brit’sh squadron in those waters and
to develop Bermuda as a dockyard
and coaling station, but it is declared
that this is merely to remedy a mis-
take that was made under Admiral
Fisher’s regime.

Bermuda, it is argued, has been a
British possession since 1609, long
before the Monroe doctrine existed,
and it became a regular coaling staL
tion in 1869, and was made the
headquarters of the North American
and West Indies squadron years ago.
Under Lord Fisher's naval redistri-
bution scheme, owing to the naval
pressure in Europe, the number of
ships that had headquarters at Ber-
muda was reduced, but the dockyard
was always maintained .

So far as is known, all that the Ad-
miralty now intends to do is to sta-
tion four armored cruisers in the
West Indies during the winter
months. No battleships are going
there.

Chicago, Ill., Aug. 5—Chicago's ten
police women, clad in modest blue
bands, went on duty today. Their
work will be mainly to look out for wo-
men and children. The details of
their duties, apparel and powers, have
been worked out by a committee of
women social workers and the chief of
police. The chief had considered as-
signing the police women to stations
in the red light districts, but it was
decided they would be of greater ser-
vice in the parks and other places of
recreation.

The police women will visit the pub-
lic dance halls, excursion boats, beach-
es and railway stations, and will try
to keep young folks off the streets
late at night. They will obtain infor-
mation rather than make arrests, al-
though on occasions they may be call-
ed upon to arres some one.

Whether the police women shall
wear brass buttons and carry revol-
vers and clubs will be decided after
the civil service examination for the
position.

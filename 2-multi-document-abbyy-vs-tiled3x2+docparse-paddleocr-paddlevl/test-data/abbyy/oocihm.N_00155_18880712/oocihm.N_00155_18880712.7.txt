The Dominion election petition came up
for trial before Judge Armour to-day.

necessary to go into them. The examina-
tion of one or two witnesses clearly proved
that the respondent owed his election to a
systematic course of bribery and corruption,
and his counsel speedily threw up the sponge,
announcing that Mr. Henderson was
willing to admit bribery by agents.
Under these circumstances the petitioner
did not press the personal charges,
though it was believed they could
have been sustained. Judge Armour there-
upon declared the election null and void,

NAPANEE, Ont., July 4.—A frame house,
occupied by Wm. Edgar, on the south side
of the river, opposite Napanee, was dis-
covered to be on fire about 5 o'clock this
afternoon. The house and contents were a5
total loss. No insurance on either. Loss

WINNIPEG, July 4.—A livery stable
belonging to one Sage was destroyed by fire
at Brandon. Loss, $2,000.

SOUTHAMPTON, July 5.—A most disastrous
fire occurred here this evening at 8 o'clock,
through which about four thousand ties,
two cars belonging to the Grand Trunk
Railway and about one hundred and fifty
feet of the south landing pier, the property
of the Government, were burned. With

the greatest difficulty the citizens pre-
vented the fire from running up the entire
length of the dock, and but for the strenu-
ous efforts made the loss would have been
many thousands of dollars. The steam
barge P. Cress had discharged her cargo of
ties just an hour or two before the fire was
discovered, at which time everything was
safe. At present nothing definite is known
as to the origin of the fire.

A meeting of the Canadian Packers
Association was held at the St. Nicholas
Hotel yesterday afternoon, Mr. Æ.Carpen-
ter, of this city, being in the chair. Mr.
Wellington Boulter, of the Bay of Quinte
Canning Factories, gave an account of his
visit to the Western Packers' Association
at their meeting in St. Louis, and submit-
ted the plan by which the members of that
association ascertain statistics regarding
the crops. Reports of the condition of the
Canadian crops were made. Peas were re-
ported to be almost a total failure in Eastern
Ontario. Mr. Fenton (St. Catharines), Mr.
Carpenter and Mr. Brown reported a very
fair crop in the Hamilton and Niagara
districts. Strawberries were light all over,
none being packed in St. Catharines and
Hamilton districts. Raspberries have been
helped very much by the rain and they
premise well. Tomatoes have been seriously
injured by the grub. Corn has also been
cut by the grub, but what has been left
standing is doing very well. Mr. J. B. Mc-
Lean was elected Secretary-Treasurer, and
was instructed to collect statistics similar
to those obtained by the Western Packers'
Association.

Daring Outrage at Toronto.

At 10.15 last (Thursday) night a dastardly
assault and robbery took place near the
Woodbine. A street car driver named
Skinner had just changed his horses at the
terminus when two men entered his car,
knocked him down, kicked him and robbed
him of his money—about $4 or $5 in silver,
and bills and tickets to the amount of $10.
The miscreants got away, leaving the poor
fellow in a pitiable plight. He was barely
able to take his car to the east end stables
and report the robbery.

Liability of Servants.

Maud Bird, a servant employed at the
Dominion House, sued Mr. Mackie for $4
wages to-day. The landlord showed that
he offered the girl her wages less certain
deductions for breakage, and the magistrate
ruled that if she thought the amount ($2)
which Mr. Mackie wanted to withhold was
too great she must replace the articles.—

He Was Afraid to Tell.

The body of a little boy, whose parents
reside at Niagara Falls, Ont., was found in
the river below the whirlpool on Wednes-
day. He had been swimming with a com-
panion above the whirlpool and was car-
ried out by the current. His comrade, a
very little boy, was frightened and told
nothing about the accident until the body
was recovered on Wednesday.

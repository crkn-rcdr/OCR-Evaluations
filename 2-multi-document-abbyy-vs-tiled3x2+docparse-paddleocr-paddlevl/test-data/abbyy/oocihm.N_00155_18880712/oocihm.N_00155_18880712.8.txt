AN AMUSING WAGER.

How Two Gentlemen Assumed the Role of
Street Organ Grinders.

On Saturday afternoon, while a number
of gentlemen, seated in front of the Queen’s
Hotel, Toronto, were being entertained by
two Italian organ grinders, some one bet
$20 that no two of the gentlemen present
would play the organ from York to Yonge
streets, on King. The bet was instantly ac-
cepted by a youngfashionable attache of the
gas works and a Chicago commercial travel-
1er. The Globe tells how they won it : Off
they started for the corner of York and
King streets, the Englishman pulling and
the Chicago man pushing the machine,
followed by the Italians and the crowd
which formed along the way. The firs
stand was made at the Rossin House,
entrance on King street, the long
slim American, full of business, turning
the handle and grinding out now a hymn
and now a selection from " Dorothy,"
while the Englishman took up a collection.
A big crowd gathered astonished to see two
well-dressed gentlemen playing a barrel-
organ. They got a big reception. Ten
and five cent pieces poured into the Eng-
lishman's straw hat, nor were other dona-
tions wanting. One young lady placed a
small bouquet of flowers on the organ,
which was politely acknowledged by the
tall American lifting his hat and taking a
more vigorous grip of theorgan handle. A
gentleman in the crowd bought a cabbage
and placed it alongside the bouquet. It
was also politely acknowledged. About
500 people gathered and the fun was great.
Slowly the machine was dragged east-
ward along King street, numerous halts
being made. The young ladies who hap-
pened to be in a certain King street restau-
rant were canvassed and respondedliberally
with silver pieces. And then a big police-
man came along and gazed at the two
musicians, and then a thought struck him.
He asked the tall man for his license and
was referred to the Englishman. Explana-
tions followed, and the big policeman,
entering into the fun of the thing, allowed
the procession to proceed. At last the cor-
ner of Yonge street was reached and the
bet was won, the organ was handed over to
its rightful owners, the cash receipts ware
counted, amounting to $6.75, and were also
given the two delighted foreigners, and the
winners of the bet, after being presented
with two handsome bouquets, returned to
the Queen's Hotel, very well pleased with
their hour's fun.

A Berlin cable says : Dr. Gerhardt,
acting under the directions of the German
doctors who attended the late Emperor
Frederick, has drawn up a report stating
that early in 1887 the German physicians
pronounced the disease from which he was
suffering to be of a cancerous nature. They
decided to open the larynx, and prepare-
tions were made to perform the operation
on May 20th when Dr. Mackinzie saw|
Emperor Frederick and declared that the I
disease was not cancer, and opposed an
operation. Dr. Mackenzie removed a
portion of the growth, which Prof. Virchow
examined, but there was no proof that the
piece was taken from the actually affected |
part. Dr. Gerhardt examined Emperor
Frederick’s throat after the piece had been
removed, and accused Dr. Mackenzie of
removing a piece from the unaffected part
of the throat instead of from the diseased
part. Dr. Mackenzie replied that it was
possible he had done so. The German |
Doctors protested against Dr. Mackenzie's
treatment throughout. Prof.Von Bergmann
corroborates Dr. Gerhardt's statement, and
gives the reason why he lost confidence in
Dr. Mackenzie.

Dr. Bergmann, in his report on the case
of Emperor Frederick, says that as early
as May, 1887, he wanted to open the
patient's larynx and excise the growth on
the left vocal chord. A small operation
promised the best results. Every such
operation he had performed in Berlin all
winter was successful. There was then no I
question of the total extirpation of the
larynx. The operation would neither have
endangered the life nor would ithave
destroyed the voice, although it would have
left it hoarse. At the time Emperor
Frederick said : "Dr. Bergmann, this
tumor must be removed. If it cannot be
done on the inside, cut from the outside.
Everything was in readiness for the opera-
tion when the plan was vetoed by Dr.
Mackenzie, who repeated that after a few
weeks of his mild treatment the Emperor
would recover his voice and be able to
command the autumn military manœuvres.
Dr. Bergmann censures Dr. Mackenzie for
defective manipulation of the larynx, for
affording no guarantee that heever reached
the growth, and for trying to shift the
responsibility upon Prof. Virchow. He
also blames Dr. Mackenzie for influencing
the press through communications about
his details of the Emperor's illness.

Daring Burglary at the Falls.

NIAGARA FALLS, Ont., July 7.—Avery
daring burglary was effected at the Wesley
Park Hotel early this morning. The
burglars effected an entrance into the
hotel, it is supposed, by opening the front
door by means of skeleton keys, and
cleaned out two rooms of their valuables
by fishing them through the transoms at
the top of the doors. Rev. Nathaniel Sites,
Chinese missionary, loses $26 in money
and $100 in jewellery and clothes. Mr.
Strawn, the proprietor of the hotel, loses
$15 in money, $600 in notes, cheque for
$83.77, and jewellery and clothes amount-
ing to $100.

The Pan-Presbyterian Council.

Presbyterian Council yesterday Rev. Mr.
Mathews, of Quebec, was elected permanent

is to meet in Toronto next. The council
had an interesting and instructive discus-

TOO MUCH ASSUMPTION.
The free trade assumption is that though free
trade would reduce wages, prices would be so
much lower that the purchasing power of money
would be greater than it is at present, and so the
workingman would be as well off as he is now, if
not better off.-Hamilton Spectator.
Upon this basis, our contemporary
proceeds to build an argument to the effect
that the reduction of wages would do the
workingman more harm than the decrease
of the price of goods would do him good.
There is really no occasion to debate that
question. The premises are wrong. The
free traders do not assume that free trade
would reduce wages. On the contrary
they contend that free trade would increase
Wages, and they appeal to both history and
reason in support of that contention.
Suppose that money wages remained the
same, and that the removal of import
duties simply caused prices of commodities.
ities to fall, the change would be
equivalent to an increase of the wages
of labor. The man earning $l a day could
support his family much more comfortably
with sugar at 5 cents a pound,
cotton at 5 cents & yard and
chairs at 50 cents apiece than with
those commodities ranging at 7, 8 and 75
cents respectively. Man does not work for
money, but for the things that money will
buy. As soon as he gets his daily or
weekly wages, he goes and trades off the
money for things that he wants more than
he wants the money, and the more of
those things he can get for a given
amount of money the better off he is...
imports, and the articles to be exported
can only be produced by labor. The
primary effect of free trade in Canada
would be to make farming profitable. Buy-
ing his supplies at low prices, and selling
his produce as such prices as now prevail,
the farmer would have a fine margin of
profit, and many people would turn to
farming. The farmer's sons, instead of going
to the city to look for jobs, would take
ap land, and even some city men would
start at farming. All the farmers would
want to trade off their surplus of food
products for stoves, furniture, implements,
cottons, etc., and the result would be a
largely increased demand for manufactured
goods accompanied by a decrease of the
men ready to make the goods. Wages go
up when two jobs are looking for one man,
and they fall when, as at present, two men
are looking for one job. Of course free
trade would raise wages in Canada, just as
it did raise wages in England forty years
ago. If this is not true; if protection can
raise wages, why is it so long setting about
it? It hasn't don't anything of the kind
yet, in this or any other country we ever
heard of.

Concert in Burlington.
A fine new organ has just been put in the
Methodist Church, Burlington, and was
opened last night by a concert and organ
recital. There was & large audience, the
church being crowded with members of the
congregation and friends, including many
from this city. The new pastor, Rev.
John Wakefield, formerly of the First
Methodist Church, this city, occupied the
chair. After the congregation had sung
" Praise Ye the Lord," the following very
excellent programme was given: Organ
solo, by Mr. L. H. Parker; chorus," The
Heavens are Telling;" solo, Mrs. Wright,
of Ancaster; organ solo, Mr. Parker; song,
Mr. Jerome, of this city; are quartette,
Messrs. Jerome, E. H. Hall, Charles and
George Cooke; solo, Miss Chittenden, of
this city; organ solo, Mr, Parker; solo, Mr.
Morley, Hamilton, Miss Chittenden
and Messrs. Jerome and Morley; chorus,
Mozart's"Gloria;"closing voluntary, Mr.
Parker. The numbers were well rendered,
and the audience wag particularly well.
pleased with the concert.

An Expensive Drive.
John McNeil, a young man, was charged
by Wm. Shaver at this morning's session
of the Police Court with having stolen a
horse. Ee pleaded not guilty. Mr. Shaver,
who lives in Ancaster, but keeps his horse
in this city, swore that he came home from
Muskoka on Saturday night and on going
to his stable found that the horse was gone.
About 11 o'clock he found it in McNeil's
possession. Other evidence, however,
showed that the young man had not in-
tended to steal the horse, but to have & a
good time with it; also that McNeil had
got it by misrepresentation. The Magis.-
trate advised him to stick to the truth in
future, and fined him $3 and $3 costs for
trespass.

A County Case.
Jobn Connors, & resident of Barton Town-ship-
was before the Police Magistrate this
morning, charged by Hannah A. Connors,
his better-half, with using indecent and
profane language and threatening to kill
her and her family. The complainant said
the charge was true in every respect, while
Connors said if the woman lived till he
struck her she would live for ever. He also
said that he had made all his property over
to hor and had not bad any peace since
doing so. He had no witnesses and was

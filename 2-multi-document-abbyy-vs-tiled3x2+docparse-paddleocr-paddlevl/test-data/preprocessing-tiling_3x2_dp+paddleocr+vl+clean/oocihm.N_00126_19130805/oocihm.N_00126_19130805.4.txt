LIBERALS SHIRKED DUTY.
How the Liberal party with its loud
protests for the good of the people
has been tried and found wanting is
written in the official at Ottawa.
There it can be seen that the late Gov-
1
ernment deliberately shirked its duty
1
to inquire into the question of ocean
freight rates though the British Gov-
ernment was willing to enter into ne-
gotiations. The Conservative opposition-
tion in 1910 time and again endeavor-
ed to have the Liberals take the mat-
ter up. There was some correspondence-
dence but the matter ended there. But
the Conservative Government has now
sent the Chairman of the Board of
Railway Commissioners to England to
negotiate and a report will be made on
his return. The late Mr. Justice Ma-
bee, chairman of the Railway Board,
and appointed by Sir Wilfrid Laurier
and a man who was respected and ad-
mired by all parties, suggested a
scheme of joint control of rates.
His memorandum was forwarded to
the Colonial Office by the then Gov-
ernment. The Imperial authorities at
once fell in wih the suggestion of a
joint investigation, but the Canadian
Government dropped the whole mat-
ter, neglecting even to reply to the
communications forwarded on the sub-
ject by the Colonial Office.
The British Government is now and
has been for some time ready to take
the ocean freight rate question up with
the Dominion Government. Records
now on file here show this and furnish
a complete refutation of the contention-
tion put by the Opposition last session
Imperial sentiment of, English speaking-
ing Canada they manifested an un-
due subservience to the Nationalists
of Quebec. At the same time. led on
by Mr. Fielding ， the Liberal leader
alarmed the true national sentiment
of the country by abandoning his dig-
nified attiture towards the United
States and by, in fact, turning back in-
lo the old American trail, and entering
into a trade arrangement which men-
aced the fiscal and perhaps in the long
run even the political integrity of the
Dominion.
As a result we had the decisive de
feat of the Government in 1911 and
ever since bye-elections and Provin.
cial general elections have in the main
:
showed progressive Liberal losses.
Only two of the men who rescued the
party in 1896 remain in the field.
They are far spent, and the leading
newspapers which support them are
so out of touch with public sentiment
1
that they still attack the National
Policy and resist the movement to-
wards closer Imperial unity. They
still preach Free Trade, Reciprocity,
and an extreme autonomy which,
though they may not recognize it,
leads towards separation from the
Empire eventually. It is difficult to
see how the fortunes of Liberalism can
improve until it develops new leaders
holding and putting into constant practice-
tice the convictions on basie National
and Imperial questions so long held
and lived by men live Sir Oliver Mow-
Et. Alexander Mackenzie and Clifford
sifton.

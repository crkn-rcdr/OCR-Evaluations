In The Gleaner yesterday the refer-
references to the remarks made by Rev.
Dr. W. H. Smith at the public meeting
geld under ie auspi es of the Labor
Council on Saturday evening were in-
advertently omitted.
Rev. Dr. Smith declared that educa-
tion, rather than agitation, was what
was necessary to bring success to the
cause of the labor unions. The solution
of the problem of the relation of the
dollar to labor and the equitable di-
vision of the profits between capital
and labor was the big question.
His
remarks were listened to with much
interest, were frequently applauded
and included statistics as to the num-
ber of men employed in various trades
for the capital invested and also as to
the percentage of increase in profits on
Investments in certain industries as
compared with the increase in wages
in recent years.
The Thursday Half Holiday.
The following is a copy of the reso-
lution passed by the Labor Council at
its last meeting relative to the Thurs-
day half holiday and referred to in
The Gleaner yesterday:
Whereas, we regard the granting of
a weekly half holiday by the merchants
of Fredericton to their employees as a
boon to our fellow workers and a
movement showing consideration on
the part of employers for those whom
they employ.
Resolved, that this Council regards
the refusal of one large firm to grant
tie half holiday as practically an ef-
fort to defeat the same, and we desire
to place on record our sympathy with
those who are promoting the holiday
movement, and pledge our support to
those merchants who have so generally-
ously considered the welfare of their
employees.

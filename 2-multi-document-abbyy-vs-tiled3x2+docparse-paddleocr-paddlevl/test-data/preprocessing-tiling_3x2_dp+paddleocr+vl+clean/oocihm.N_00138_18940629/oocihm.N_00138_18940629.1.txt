The Colonial Conference met in secret
conclave in the office of the Minister of
Trade and Commerce this forenoon. All
the delegates were present. Hon. Mac-
kenzie Bowell was appointed President of
the Conference and Sir Adolphe Caron was
appointed Vice-President. It is under-
stood that the whole of the forenoon
session was taken up with the work of
oranization. Mr. Bowell read a very
carefully prepared basis, in which it was
proposed that the Conference should pro-
ceed. This was adopted. On this basis
it was arranged that tie question of
the Pacific cable should be taken up on
Monday next.
A resolution was also passed express-
ing sympathy with Mi. Bowell, owing to
the death of his son-in-law, Mr. George
McCarthy. The funeral takes place to-
morrow in Belleville, and Mr. Bowell
leaves for there to-night. The delegates
being tired after last night's banquet,
decided to adjourn for to-day at half-
past one o'clock.
J. L. Payne, private secretary for Mr.
Bowell, and Douglas Stewart, private
secretary to Sir John Thompson, were

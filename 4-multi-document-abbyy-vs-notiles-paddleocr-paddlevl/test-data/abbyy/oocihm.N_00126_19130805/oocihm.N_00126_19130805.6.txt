Chicago, Aug. 4—The pace set By
Joe Jackson proved too swift for Ty
Cobb last week, and the Detroit player
fell behind in the American League
batting averages. Jackson pounded
his per centage up to .408, but Cobb
dropped to .396. The Cleveland man
has played in 93 games, while Cobb
has been in only 69.

Dan Murphy, Philadelphia, is third
in the American League with .378.
Then come Speaker, Boston, with .371;
Caldwell New York, .355; Henriksen,
Boston, .351; Collins, Philadelphia,
.348; McInnes, Philadelphia, .333;
Gandil, Washington, .332; and Lalole,
Cleveland, who ranks 10th with .321.

McDonald Leads in National.

Charles McDonald, Boston, con-
tinues to be the real leader in the Nat-
ional with .371, though Earl Yingling,
Brooklyn, for 23 games, is hitting
.393. Hyatt, Pittsburg, is third with
362; R. Miller, Philadelphia, fourth
with .359, and Daubert, Brooklyn, fifth,
with 349. Then comes Walsh, Phila-
delphia, .346; Cravath, Philadelphia,
.341; W. Collins, Boston, .333; Zimmer-
man, Chicago, .318, and Hess, Boston,
.317.'

The Pitching Leaders.

Pitching honors to date show Wag-
ner, of Brooklyn, the only undefeated
hurler in the National League, with
two games to his credit, though he
has taken part in eleven. Humphries,
Chicago, and Demaree, New York are
tied, with nine wins and two defeats.
Mathewson, New York, is next, with
18 victories and 5 defeats, while Mar-
quard. New York, is fifth with 14 wins
and 5 defeats.

Boehling, of Washington, who suf-
fered his first defeat the other day, is
ahead among the American League
pitchers, with 11 victories and one de-
feat up to the time of closing the un-
official averages for the week. His
team mate, Walter Johnson, ranks
next, with 23 games won and 5 lost.
Then comes Bender, Philadelphia with
16 won and 5 lost; Plank, Philadel-
phia, with 15 and 5, and Brown. Phila-
delphla, with 13 and 6.

conducting a lottery in the form of
a baseball pool, with a list of 3,900 cus-

charged 20 cents for tickets on the re-

leagues. A sum was paid to the win.
ners having high or low daily or week
ly scores, it is said.

The Police Department has an
nounced its purpose of breaking up the
practice of gambling in baseball pools.

Pitcher Jack Conley of the St. Croix
Club has left to join the Lynn Club,
and if lie makes good it is said the
price paid for his release will be $300
or $400.

Conley was with McKeesport, Pa.,
last year and is a big fellow. The
players of the N. B. & Maine circuit
seem to feel that if he is lacking in
anything to make good in the New
England circuit it will be in stamina.
He has a deceptive, low-breaking spit-
ter that is very effective when it is
working right.

Charlie O’Brien, the St. John club's
third baseman, will probably be back
in the game again this week, being now
well on the way to recovery from the
effects of being hit by a ball thrown
by Dave Morey to catch him off first
at St. John more than a week ago. The
little fellow is one of the most aggres-
sive players in the country and his
return will likely result in McPherson
being shifted to shortstop. O'Brien ex-
pects to be playing on Friday.

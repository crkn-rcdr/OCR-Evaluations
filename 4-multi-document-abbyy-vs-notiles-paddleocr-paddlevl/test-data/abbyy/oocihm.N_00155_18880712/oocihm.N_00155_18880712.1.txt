THE FIRE RECORD.,

Big Blaze at Bowmanville-Old Man
Burned to Death.

BELLEVILLE, July 8.—On Saturday after-
noon an old man named Mathew Leonard,
residing on the 9th concession of Tyendi-
naga, went out to fire some stumps. Some
time later a neighbor passing found the
fence burning and began to tear it down.
While so engaged he heard groans, and, on
looking about, discovered Leonard lying on
the ground with his clothing burnt nearly
off and his legs and the lower part of his
body burnt almost to a cinder. Assistance
was at once obtained, but too late, and
Leonard shortly after breathed his last.
How the accident occurred will never be
known, but it is surmised that he fell in a
fit, and when his clothing caught fire was
unable to help himself, and so perished,
after suffering the most terrible agony.

them to-day enveloped the river and Bay
of Quinte and interfered with the naviga-
tion of them.

SAULT STE. MARIE, Mich., July 7.—The
farm house of George Dobbs, located seven
miles from here,on the Canadian side of the
river, was burned early yesterday morn-
ing. Four of the inmates, George Dobbs,
aged 72; David Merrifield, aged 21 ; Alice
Thomson, aged 9, and another child,
perished in the flames. The fire started
rom a smudge and when discovered by
Mrs. Dobbs, who is 70 years old, it, was
beyond control. Mrs. Dobbs quickly
awoke her husband and Mr. Merrifield, and
descended to the lower floor. While groping
about in the stifling smoke she stumbled
and fell through a trap door into the cellar
and from there made her escape to the
open air. The two men failed to find any
means of exit, fell back into the flames and
were consumed. The children were asleep
in another part of the house and were
suffocated. The woman, who had no time
to put on any clothing, lay exposed to the
chilly air until morning, when her cries
attracted the attention of a neighbor.
There is little hope for her recovery.

A MORMON BISHOP.

How a Salt Lake Refugee Surprised the
Citizens of Lunenberg, N.S.

A Lunenberg, N.S., despatch says :
Chester has had during the past week a
genuine sensation. About six weeks ago
there arrived from parts unknown a white-
haired old patriarch and three very
vivacious young ladies. One was introduced
as his betterhalf, the others as his wife’s
sisters. The ladies were invited every-
where, and when not accompanied by the old
man had a good time. About a week ago
the youngest became infatuated with one
of our young men, and, in a moment of con-
fidence, disclosed the fact that the old
patriarch was a Mormon bishop, a refugee
from Salt Lake, Utah, and that all the
women were, according to the rites of the
Mormon Church, his wives. The next thing
to an impossibility is to keep a secret in
Chester, so the whole matter leaked out.
The ‘bishop " got wind of the affair, and
with his three wives skipped out on Satur-
day night at 10 o’clock for Windsor.

Brantford News Notes.

BRANTFORD, July 8.—At a special meet-
ing of the Public School Board held Friday
evening it was decided to introduce the
kindergarten system in Brantford. Mrs.
Wylie, of Toronto, late of Chicago, was
engaged as instructress and directress out
of a large number of applicants.

George Leach, a member of a respectable
family, but himself a dissolute character,
was fined on Friday at the Police Court $5
or two months, and given time to pay.
Leach celebrated his liberty by forging hisI
brother's name to an order, and securing a
ham from a merchant named Cammell.
This ham he sold for 75 cents to a boarding
house keeper, and then again got on a
drunk. He was re-arrested Friday even-
ing, and yesterday morning sentenced to
22 months in Central Prison.

Before Judge Jones yesterday afternoon
at 3 o'clock George Campbell was arraigned
to receive sentence on a chargeof attempted
rape of a Hamilton girl. His Honor at first
sentenced Campbell to four months' im-
prisonment, but before the sentence was
recorded it was pointed out by the Crown
Attorney that the Consolidated Statutes
made the offence punishable by a sentence
of not less than two years nor more than
seven. The judge, having at first pro-
nounced sentence acting on the old statute,
decided to let matters remain in statu quo
until he can communicate with the Minister
of Justice and see whether there is not a
clerical error in the Consolidated Statutes.

KINGSTON, July 9.—The Rathbuns, of
Deseronto, lose about $35,000 by the de-
struction of the chemical works on Sunday.
It was probably a case of spontaneous
combustion. The works, which have been
burned the second time, will be rebuilt at
once of terra cotta. The manufacture of
chemicals is now paying handsomely, but
only after great expenditure in perfecting
the system of distillation.

LINDSAY, July 9.—On Saturday evening a
very disastrous fire raged at Corson’s sid-
ing, on the Midland Railway, destroying
the station and everything in the vicinity
of it. The property destroyed consisted of
4,000 telegraph poles, an immense quantity
of cedar posts and ties, several thousand
cords of wood, a large quantity of lumber,
together with Decatur’s steam saw mill
and outbuildings, also Captain Corson’s
coal-house, with several hundred bushels of
coal. The loss is roughly estimated at
$10,000, and so far as can be learned there
is no insurance. The fire was caused by a
spark being carried by the wind from bush
fires two miles distant.

sock becoming saturated with blood, some

the prison clothes. He lost 50 lbs. in weight-
while in prison, and was broken down in

Drought in Eastern Ontario.

KINGSTON, July 9.—The country in this
section and in Hastings, Lennox and Ad-
dington, Frontenac, Prince Edward and
Leeds is suffering on account of drought.
The hay crop is a perfect failure. Prince
Edward farmers estimate that they will
have to import 1,000 tons for their own use.
Other crops, even if rain should now come,
will only be one-third of the average.
Bush fires in some places continue to do
great damage to crops and fencing. The
situation is critical for the farmer, and it
is viewed with alarm by business men.

Smothered In a Sand Heap.

MONTREAL, July 9.—A young boy
named D. Burns, 6 years of age,
disappeared from his home Saturday
on Clemen street. Search was
made all day yesterday, but without success
till this morning,when his dead body was
found in a big heap of sand at the end of
Ontario street. He had been smothered to
death.

Serious Charge Against a Contractor.

SARNIA, July 10.—Chief Sherwood, of the
Secret Service Department, with an
assistant, drove to Thedford during last
night and arrested W. O. Parsons, a
prominent contractor, on a charge of
counterfeiting. Parsons was brought here
this morning and remanded for one week.
There was found in his house and on his
person about $600 in bills and American
silver. Parsons is alleged to be mixed up
in the counterfeiting work on which Chas.
Johnston, now in jail here, is held.
